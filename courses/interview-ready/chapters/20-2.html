The signal

"Walk me through a project on your CV": the deep dive. A senior engineer picks one item and keeps asking "why?" and "how did you know?" for 20–40 minutes. It's the round where your GPU course labs and projects pay off, provided you can explain every decision and number.

The core idea: be ready three levels deep

  1. Level 1, the overview (1 minute): the problem, why it mattered, what you built, and the headline result.
  2. Level 2, the design (5 minutes): the architecture or algorithm, key choices and the alternatives you rejected.
  3. Level 3, the depths (on demand): exact measurements and tools, the bug that took longest, the limits of your solution, what you'd do with more time, how it would scale.

Prepare a one-page sheet per project with: a diagram you can draw in 60 seconds, five key numbers, three design decisions with alternatives, two things that went wrong, and two things you'd change.

The invariant and template

Project:          e.g. "Tiled GEMM and FlashAttention-style kernel on an RTX 2060 Super"
One-liner:        problem → approach → result (with a number)
Diagram:          data flow / memory hierarchy / threads
Key numbers:      baseline, final, peak, % of roofline, problem sizes
Decisions:        tile sizes (why), shared vs registers, async copies, precision
Went wrong:       bank conflicts; register spills at large tiles (how detected, how fixed)
Limits:           what doesn't scale; where it's still memory-bound
Next:             tensor cores / wider problem sizes / autotuning

A thinking trace: surviving \"why that tile size?\"

  1. Weak answer: \"it was the fastest.\" Follow-up: \"why?\", and you're stuck.
  2. Strong answer, from first principles: the tile was chosen so the working set (A and B tiles) fits in shared memory with room for 2 blocks per SM (occupancy); the arithmetic intensity per tile clears the ridge point of the roofline, so the kernel becomes compute-bound; and register pressure at larger tiles caused spills (seen as local-memory traffic in the profiler).
  3. Evidence: \"I swept 32/64/128 and measured: 64 was best; at 128 the spills cost 20%.\"
  4. Honesty about limits: \"cuBLAS still beats it by 25% at large sizes, mostly from tensor cores and better pipelining.\"

The habit: every number on your CV should have a \"because\" and a \"how I measured it\".

Traps and variations

  • Claiming more than you did: the deep dive will find it.
  • Getting lost in detail at level 1: start high, then let the interviewer steer.
  • No diagram: draw the data flow early, since it anchors the conversation.
  • Not knowing the baseline: \"3× faster\" means nothing without \"than what, measured how\".
