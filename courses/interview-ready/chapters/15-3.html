The signal

Some online assessments (and a few firms' numerical tests) include "next number in the sequence" and numerical-reasoning questions under heavy time pressure. They're pattern-recognition drills with a small catalogue of tricks, so learning the catalogue pays off quickly.

The core idea: a fixed checklist, cheapest test first

  1. Differences: first, second, third differences (polynomial sequences become constant).
  2. Ratios: geometric growth, or ratios that themselves form a sequence (×2, ×3, ×4 → factorial-like).
  3. Interleaved sequences: odd and even positions following separate rules.
  4. Recurrences: each term from the previous two (Fibonacci-like: aₙ = aₙ₋₁ + aₙ₋₂, or aₙ = aₙ₋₁ × aₙ₋₂, or + n).
  5. Special sets: primes, squares, cubes, triangular numbers n(n + 1)/2, powers of 2, factorials, n² ± 1, digit sums.
  6. Operations alternating: +3, ×2, +3, ×2…

Spend at most ~30 seconds on one question: guess using the most plausible rule and move on.

The invariant and template

Sequence  2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ?
Diff      4, 6, 8, 10        → second diff constant 2 → next diff 12 → 42   (n(n+1))
Sequence  1, 2, 6, 24, 120, ?
Ratio     2, 3, 4, 5         → next ratio 6 → 720                          (n!)
Sequence  3, 4, 7, 11, 18, ?
Rule      a(n) = a(n−1) + a(n−2) → 29                                       (Lucas)

A thinking trace: 1, 4, 9, 61, 52, 63, ?

  1. Differences: 3, 5, 52, −9, 11. No pattern.
  2. Ratios: no.
  3. Special sets: 1, 4, 9 are squares; is 61 a reversed 16? Is 52 a reversed 25, 63 a reversed 36? Yes.
  4. Answer: 49 reversed = 94.

The habit: when arithmetic tests fail, look at the digits (reversals, digit sums, concatenations). It's the last item on the checklist for a reason.

Traps and variations

  • Several valid rules can fit a short sequence; choose the simplest (lowest description length).
  • Numerical reasoning tables (percent changes, ratios from charts) are just Chapter 15.1's arithmetic under time pressure.
  • Logic grids and \"which statement must be true\": practise a few to learn the format; don't over-invest.
