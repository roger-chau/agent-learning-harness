The signal

"Break a stick at two random points: probability the pieces form a triangle?", "three random points on a circle: probability the triangle is acute?", "Buffon's needle", "two people arrive at random within an hour and wait 15 minutes: probability they meet?". Geometric probability rewards drawing the sample space and computing an area ratio.

The core idea: probability = favourable area / total area

With uniform continuous variables, the sample space is a square, cube or simplex, and the event is a region inside it. Draw it. Most interview answers are areas of triangles in a unit square.

  • Meeting problem: arrivals X, Y uniform on [0, 1]; they meet if |X − Y| ≤ t. The complement is two corner triangles of total area (1 − t)², so P = 1 − (1 − t)². For t = ¼: 7/16.
  • Broken stick: a triangle needs every piece < ½. The favourable region is the medial triangle, ¼ of the area, so P = ¼.
  • Circle points (Wendel): n random points lie in some semicircle with probability n/2ⁿ⁻¹; three points form an acute triangle iff they're not in a semicircle: 1 − 3/4 = ¼.
  • Buffon: a needle of length l ≤ d on lines spaced d apart crosses a line with probability 2l/(πd).

The invariant and template

1. Name the random variables and their joint uniform domain (square, cube, simplex, angle × offset).
2. Translate the event into inequalities; DRAW the region.
3. Use symmetry to cut the region (e.g. assume X < Y and double).
4. Area by triangles and rectangles; integrate only if forced.
5. Check: P in [0, 1]; limiting cases (t → 0, t → 1).

A thinking trace: points in a semicircle

n points uniform on a circle. Probability that they all lie in some semicircle?

  1. Find the right events: for each point i, let Aᵢ = "all other points lie in the semicircle starting at i going clockwise".
  2. P(Aᵢ): each other point independently falls in that half with probability ½, so (½)ⁿ⁻¹.
  3. Disjoint: if all points fit in a semicircle, exactly one point is the clockwise-first of the arrangement (probability-1 genericity), so at most one Aᵢ happens.
  4. Sum: P = n/2ⁿ⁻¹. For n = 3 that's ¾, so P(acute) = ¼; n = 4 gives ½.

The habit: split the event into disjoint pieces indexed by a canonical choice (the first point, the largest element) to avoid inclusion–exclusion.

Traps and variations

  • Bertrand's paradox: "a random chord" is ambiguous; different uniform models give 1/2, 1/3 or 1/4. Ask which distribution is meant.
  • Uniform on a disc isn't uniform in radius: P(R ≤ r) = r², E[R] = 2/3.
  • Distance between two uniform points on [0, 1]: E|X − Y| = 1/3.
  • Monte Carlo π: P(a point in the unit square lands inside the quarter circle) = π/4. The standard error shrinks like 1/√n, which is why Monte Carlo pricing needs variance reduction.
