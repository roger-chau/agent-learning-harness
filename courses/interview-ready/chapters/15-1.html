The signal

Many trading firms start with a timed arithmetic test (Optiver's 80 questions in 8 minutes; Flow Traders, IMC, Akuna and others run similar screens), and mental maths keeps coming up in later rounds: "quick, what's 17% of 340?", "what's 0.35 × 48?", while you're also juggling a game. The bar is accuracy at speed, and that's trainable. Do the drill below for 10 minutes most days for a few weeks and your score will climb steadily.

The core idea: never do the hard multiplication; rewrite it into easy ones

  • Decompose: 7 × 86 = 7 × 80 + 7 × 6 = 560 + 42 = 602.
  • Round and compensate: 49 × 6 = 50 × 6 − 6 = 294; 298 + 467 = 300 + 467 − 2 = 765.
  • Difference of squares: 47 × 53 = 50² − 3² = 2,491. Any product of two numbers equally far from a round number.
  • Squares near 50 and 100: (50 + k)² = 2,500 + 100k + k², so 53² = 2,809. (100 − k)² = 10,000 − 200k + k², so 97² = 9,409. And numbers ending in 5: 65² = 6 × 7 then append 25 = 4,225.
  • × 5, × 25, × 125: × 10 ÷ 2, × 100 ÷ 4, × 1000 ÷ 8. So 36 × 25 = 900.
  • Percentages are symmetric: x% of y = y% of x. 8% of 25 = 25% of 8 = 2. For 17% of 340, do 10% + 5% + 2% = 34 + 17 + 6.8 = 57.8.
  • Fractions you must know as decimals: eighths (0.125, 0.375, 0.625, 0.875), sixths (0.1667, 0.8333), sevenths (1/7 = 0.142857, and the others rotate the same digits), ninths (0.111…), 1/16 = 0.0625.
  • Divide by simplifying first: 336 ÷ 7 = 48; 4,200 ÷ 35 = 120 (both ÷ 35 → 8,400 ÷ 70).

The invariant and template: practise here

How to train: alternate a 2-minute arithmetic run (it builds raw speed) with a trading maths run (percentages, fractions, decimal products, near-square products, expected values). Read the per-type table after each run, practise the slowest type with the tricks above, and log your best scores. A reasonable target before applying: 40+ in 2 minutes of arithmetic with no skips, and steady accuracy in trading maths.

A thinking trace: 0.35 × 48 under time pressure

  1. See the structure: 0.35 = 7/20. So 48 × 7 / 20.
  2. Do the easy part first: 48 / 4 = 12, so 48 / 20 = 2.4.
  3. Multiply: 2.4 × 7 = 16.8.
  4. Alternative: 0.35 × 48 = 0.35 × 50 − 0.35 × 2 = 17.5 − 0.7 = 16.8. Two routes agree.
  5. Magnitude check: roughly ⅓ of 48 is 16, so 16.8 is plausible.

The habit: convert decimals to fractions when the fraction is friendly (0.125, 0.25, 0.35 = 7/20, 0.375, 0.6 = 3/5, 0.75), and always check the order of magnitude.

Traps and variations

  • Accuracy beats speed on tests that penalise wrong answers; know the scoring before you start.
  • Don't stall on one question. Skip after about 5 seconds, since every question is worth the same.
  • Write nothing down in your head: keep at most one intermediate number in working memory by choosing decompositions that finish quickly.
  • Negative numbers and order of operations are where fast people lose points.
  • Fatigue: an 8-minute test is long. Practise full-length runs too, not only 2-minute sprints.
