The signal

Classic brainteasers still appear, especially in first rounds and with senior traders who like them: weighings, burning ropes, hats and prisoners, pirates splitting gold, crossing bridges. There's a small set of recurring ideas; recognising which one applies is most of the work.

The core idea: six recurring ideas

IdeaClassic
Information counting: each test has k outcomes, so n tests distinguish at most kⁿ cases12 coins, one counterfeit (heavier or lighter): 3 weighings, 3³ = 27 ≥ 24 cases
Backward induction from the last decisionpirates dividing gold; the last-person-standing games
Invariants and parity: find a quantity that never changesa chessboard with two opposite corners removed can't be tiled by dominoes (colour count)
Symmetry and pairinga strategy-stealing argument; pair up moves to mirror an opponent
Encoding / shared strategyhats in a line (parity of what you see), 100 prisoners and boxes (follow the cycle)
Extreme or small cases firsttry n = 1, 2, 3 and spot the pattern; then prove it

The invariant and template

1. Restate precisely; ask about edge rules.
2. Solve tiny cases (n = 1, 2, 3) out loud.
3. Ask which idea applies: counting outcomes? backward induction? invariant? encoding?
4. Propose a strategy; test it on a small case; then argue why it's optimal (a lower bound).
5. Narrate throughout: interviewers grade the search, not just the answer.

A thinking trace: 100 prisoners and 100 boxes

Each prisoner may open 50 of 100 boxes to find their own number; all must succeed or all die; no communication after the start. Find a strategy with a decent success chance.

  1. Naive: random boxes, (½)¹⁰⁰ ≈ 0.
  2. Encoding idea: treat the contents as a permutation. Prisoner k opens box k, then the box numbered by what's inside, and so on, following the cycle that contains k.
  3. When does it work? Prisoner k succeeds iff k's cycle has length ≤ 50. Everyone succeeds iff the permutation has no cycle longer than 50.
  4. Probability: a random permutation of 100 has a cycle of length L > 50 with probability 1/L (at most one such cycle can exist). So P(fail) = Σ_{L=51}^{100} 1/L ≈ ln 2 ≈ 0.69, and P(success) ≈ 0.31.
  5. The insight: the strategy makes the prisoners' successes highly correlated, so they all succeed or fail together, instead of independently.

The habit: when individual chances are fixed but you need everyone to succeed, look for a strategy that correlates the outcomes.

Traps and variations

  • Answer first, then explain? No: explain your search, briefly.
  • Memorised answers get follow-ups (\"now with 13 coins\"), so know why they work.
  • Burning ropes: lighting both ends halves the time, whatever the burn rate along the rope.
  • Two-envelope paradox: \"switch because E = 1.25x\" is wrong. It conditions inconsistently on x.
  • Know when to stop: if stuck, say what you've tried and ask for a hint. That's normal and scored kindly.
