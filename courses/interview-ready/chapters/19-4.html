The signal

Knowing what the quant-dev loop looks like lets you prepare deliberately rather than broadly. Typical structure: an online assessment (Track A level, sometimes with C++-specific questions), one or two coding rounds, a C++/systems deep dive, a system design round (a trading system component), light probability, and a behavioural/fit conversation, often with a project deep dive.

Your differentiator is performance engineering: GPU kernels, profiling, roofline reasoning. Prepare two stories where you measured, found the bottleneck, fixed it, and quantified the result (Part 20 shows how).

The invariant and template: a 6-week plan before applying

Weeks 1–2:  Track A review: one Part per two days; LeetCode lists; mock OA 1
Week 3:     Parts 9–10 (C++, systems); re-implement unique_ptr, SPSC ring, order book in C++
Week 4:     Part 19 tasks end to end in C++ with benchmarks; Part 11 design practice aloud
Week 5:     Parts 13–14 probability (light); daily mental-maths drill; mock OA 2
Week 6:     Part 20 story bank; two full mock loops with a friend (coding + design + behavioural)
Daily:      2 timed coding problems, 10 min drill, 1 C++ question explained aloud

A thinking trace: the take-home

\"Write a program that reads a day of market data and outputs per-minute bars and the top of book at each bar's close. Optimise for speed. You have 4 hours.\"

  1. Clarify in writing: input format, size, time zone, how to handle gaps and crossed books. State your assumptions in a README if you can't ask.
  2. Correct first: a simple, readable version (the judged problems here); unit tests on edge cases; a test comparing against a brute-force reference on random data.
  3. Then fast: profile; parse with a hand-written fast reader rather than iostreams; avoid per-line allocation; reserve containers; benchmark and report the numbers (throughput in messages per second, before and after).
  4. Presentation: a README with the design, complexity, assumptions, test strategy, benchmark table, and what you'd do with more time. Reviewers read the README first.
  5. Don't: over-engineer, skip tests, or submit something that doesn't build from a clean checkout.

Traps and variations

  • Silence in coding rounds: quant-dev interviewers still grade communication (Part 0).
  • Over-claiming on your CV: every bullet can become a 20-minute deep dive; keep only what you can defend in detail.
  • Ignoring probability: even dev roles ask a few expectation questions. Keep Part 13 warm.
  • Not asking about the team: HFT dev roles vary enormously (strategy infrastructure, exchange connectivity, research platforms); ask which.
