The signal

Quant researcher and derivatives-desk interviews ask: "What is E[W_t²]?", "apply Itô's lemma to W_t³", "what's the distribution of S_T under geometric Brownian motion?", "why is the median of a stock price below its mean?". You need fluent mechanics, not measure theory: Brownian motion's key properties and Itô's lemma as a tool.

The core idea: (dW)² = dt

Brownian motion W: W₀ = 0, independent increments, W_t − W_s ~ N(0, t − s), continuous paths. It's the limit of a scaled random walk (Part 14.2). Its paths are so rough that the sum of squared increments over [0, t] converges to t (the quadratic variation). In calculus terms, (dW)² = dt, while dW·dt = 0 and (dt)² = 0.

Itô's lemma is the Taylor expansion that keeps that second-order term. For f(t, X_t) with dX = a dt + b dW:

df = (∂f/∂t + a ∂f/∂x + ½ b² ∂²f/∂x²) dt + b ∂f/∂x dW

  • d(W²) = 2W dW + dt, so E[W_t²] = t (Itô integrals have mean zero).
  • GBM: dS = μS dt + σS dW. Apply Itô to ln S: d ln S = (μ − σ²/2) dt + σ dW, so S_T = S₀ exp((μ − σ²/2)T + σW_T): lognormal.
  • E[S_T] = S₀e^{μT}, but the median is S₀e^{(μ − σ²/2)T}, lower. Volatility drags compounded growth: the "volatility tax" σ²/2.

The invariant and template

Itô multiplication table:   dW·dW = dt,   dW·dt = 0,   dt·dt = 0
Itô integral:               E[∫ f dW] = 0,   Var[∫ f dW] = ∫ E[f²] dt     (Itô isometry)
GBM solution:               S_T = S₀ · exp((μ − σ²/2) T + σ √T Z),   Z ~ N(0, 1)
Useful moments:             E[e^{σW_t}] = e^{σ²t/2},   E[W_t⁴] = 3t²

A thinking trace: "compute E[W_t³] and E[∫₀ᵗ W_s dW_s]"

  1. E[W_t³]: W_t ~ N(0, t), which is symmetric, so all odd moments are 0. No calculus needed: say the symmetry argument first.
  2. Itô route as a check: d(W³) = 3W² dW + 3W dt. Taking expectations, dE[W³] = 3E[W] dt = 0. ✓
  3. E[∫ W dW]: an Itô integral of an adapted integrand has mean 0.
  4. Closed form: from d(W²) = 2W dW + dt, ∫₀ᵗ W dW = (W_t² − t)/2, with mean (t − t)/2 = 0 ✓. Ordinary calculus would say W_t²/2; the −t/2 is the Itô correction.
  5. Variance of that integral (Itô isometry): ∫₀ᵗ E[W_s²] ds = t²/2.

The habit: try symmetry and known distributions first, Itô second, and use each to check the other.

Traps and variations

  • Forgetting the ½σ² term is the classic mistake (and it's why log returns and arithmetic returns differ).
  • Brownian motion isn't differentiable: dW/dt doesn't exist; work with dW.
  • Reflection principle: P(max_{s≤t} W_s ≥ a) = 2P(W_t ≥ a). It prices barrier options and answers hitting questions.
  • Brownian bridge: conditioned on W_T, the intermediate values are Gaussian with reduced variance (used in Monte Carlo path refinement).
