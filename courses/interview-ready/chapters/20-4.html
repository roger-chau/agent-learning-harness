The signal

An offer arrives, sometimes with a deadline. Negotiation is expected at almost every firm, and a single polite, well-prepared conversation is often worth more than months of salary growth. This chapter covers what's negotiable and how to ask. It isn't legal or tax advice.

The core idea: information and alternatives

  • Know the components: base salary, sign-on bonus, annual bonus (target and history), equity (type, vesting schedule, refreshers; for private companies, valuation and liquidity), level/title, start date, relocation, remote policy. At trading firms, the bonus is often the largest and most variable part. Ask how it's determined.
  • Market data: levels.fyi and peer conversations for big tech; trading-firm comp is less public, so talk to people.
  • Alternatives create leverage: competing offers (real ones) are the strongest lever. Time your processes so offers arrive close together.
  • Level matters most long-term: a higher level changes the whole band and future growth; negotiate level before numbers when your interviews support it.

The invariant and template: the conversation

1. Thank them; express genuine enthusiasm.
2. Don't accept on the call; ask for the offer in writing and for time to review.
3. Ask clarifying questions (bonus history, refreshers, vesting, level).
4. Counter once, specifically and with a reason:
   "I'm excited about the team. I have a competing offer at [total] and my research suggests
    [level/band]. If you could get the total to [number], I'd be ready to sign."
5. Get the final terms in writing.  Never invent offers or numbers.

A thinking trace: an exploding offer

  1. Situation: a trading firm offers with a 48-hour deadline; a big-tech process is two weeks from an offer.
  2. Ask for time, politely and specifically: \"I'm very interested; I'm completing another process and want to make a well-informed decision. Could I have until [date]?\" Extensions are often granted.
  3. Accelerate the other process: tell them you have an offer with a deadline and ask whether the timeline can move. Most companies can speed up.
  4. Decide on fit, not just numbers: team, learning, the work itself, career trajectory. The expected value over years, not the first-year package.
  5. If you must decide early: decide, and don't renege on an accepted offer; it damages your reputation in small industries.

Traps and variations

  • Naming your number first anchors low. When asked early about expectations, deflect politely until there's an offer, or give a well-researched range.
  • Negotiating by email only can be fine; be clear and warm either way.
  • Equity valuation: private-company equity is uncertain; value it conservatively.
  • Non-competes (common at trading firms) affect your future options; read them and ask about the paid garden-leave terms.

Practice and reading
