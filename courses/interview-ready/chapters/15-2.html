The signal

"How many piano tuners are in Chicago?", "how much does it cost to run all of the world's GPUs for a day?", "how many golf balls fit in a plane?". Fermi questions test structured reasoning under uncertainty and comfort with orders of magnitude, which traders and engineers use constantly ("is this trade worth it?", "will this fit in memory?").

The core idea: decompose into factors you can bound, then multiply logs

  1. Decompose the quantity into a product (or sum) of factors you have some feel for.
  2. Estimate each factor with a low and a high bound you'd bet on; use the geometric mean √(low × high) as the point estimate (it's the midpoint in log space).
  3. Multiply in powers of ten: keep a mantissa and an exponent; round mantissas to 1, 2, 3 or 5.
  4. Sanity-check from a second decomposition (supply side vs demand side).
  5. State the uncertainty: "about 3 × 10⁴, probably within a factor of 3".

Errors partly cancel: if each of 5 factors is within 2× and the errors are independent, the product is typically within about 2^√5 ≈ 4.7×, not 2⁵ = 32×.

The invariant and template

Useful anchors: world population 8×10⁹ · US 3.3×10⁸ · seconds/year 3.15×10⁷ ≈ π×10⁷
                Earth's surface 5×10⁸ km² · speed of light 3×10⁸ m/s · a day 8.64×10⁴ s
                US GDP ~2.8×10¹³ $ · a household ~2.5 people · a working year ~2,000 hours
log₁₀ shortcuts: log 2 ≈ 0.30, log 3 ≈ 0.48, log 5 ≈ 0.70, log 7 ≈ 0.85

A thinking trace: daily electricity for all data-centre GPUs

  1. Decompose: number of GPUs × power per GPU (plus cooling overhead) × 24 h × price per kWh.
  2. GPUs: accelerator shipments are a few million per year, with several years of installed base, so 10⁷ within a factor of 3.
  3. Power: 300–1,000 W each, geometric mean ~550 W; with a PUE of ~1.3, call it 700 W.
  4. Energy per day: 10⁷ × 0.7 kW × 24 h ≈ 1.7 × 10⁸ kWh.
  5. Cost: at ~$0.08/kWh that's 1.3 × 10⁷, about $13M per day (about $5B per year).
  6. Cross-check: 1.7 × 10⁸ kWh/day is ~7 GW average, a handful of large power plants. Plausible given current data-centre build-out news.

The habit: bounds → geometric mean → powers of ten → a cross-check from an independent angle.

Traps and variations

  • Unit errors (kW vs kWh, per day vs per year) are the most common mistake. Carry the units through every step.
  • False precision: \"17,482\" signals that you don't understand the uncertainty.
  • Silence: narrate the decomposition; the interviewer grades the structure.
  • Market-sizing variants (\"how big is the market for X?\") add penetration and price factors.
  • Trading variant: \"make me a market on the number of …\": give a bid/ask whose width reflects your uncertainty (Part 16).
