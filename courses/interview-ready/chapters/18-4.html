The signal

"You're long a delta-hedged option: when do you make money?", "what is implied volatility?", "why is there a volatility smile?", "explain gamma scalping". These separate people who memorised Black–Scholes from people who could run an options book.

The core idea: a delta-hedged option is a bet on realised vs implied volatility

Hedging a long option every Δt, your P&L per step is about

½ Γ S² (realised move² − σ_implied² Δt)

Gamma pays you when the stock moves more than implied; theta charges you the implied amount each day. Over the option's life, a long option position that's delta-hedged earns roughly ½ΓS²(σ²_realised − σ²_implied) integrated over time. That's why options traders talk in volatility, not price.

  • Implied volatility: the σ that makes Black–Scholes match the market price. It's a quoting convention: options are priced in vol.
  • The smile / skew: implied vol varies with strike. Equity index puts carry higher vol (crash risk, demand for protection); this reflects fat tails and jumps that Black–Scholes ignores.
  • Term structure: implied vol varies with maturity, often spiking before events (earnings).

The invariant and template

Daily breakeven move for a long ATM option:  σ_implied / √252  (e.g. 16% vol → 1% per day)
Hedged P&L over a step:   ½ Γ S² [(ΔS/S)² − σ²_implied Δt]
Vega P&L:   vega × Δσ_implied
"Rule of 16": annual vol ÷ 16 ≈ daily move (√256 = 16)

A thinking trace: gamma scalping a long straddle

  1. Setup: long an ATM straddle at 20% implied vol, delta-hedged, S = 100. Breakeven daily move ≈ 20%/16 = 1.25%.
  2. Day 1: the stock moves +2%. Delta rises (gamma), so you sell shares to rebalance, locking in profit from the move.
  3. Day 2: −2%. Delta falls, so you buy back shares lower. Selling high and buying low is the gamma scalp.
  4. Net: moves of 2% exceed the 1.25% breakeven, so the scalping profits beat the theta paid. You're effectively long realised vol at 20% and it realised about 32%.
  5. Risks: realised vol below implied (theta wins), implied vol falling (vega loss on the remaining position), discrete-hedging noise, transaction costs.

The habit: translate any options position into \"long or short realised vol, long or short implied vol, and at what levels\".

Traps and variations

  • \"Options are cheap because the price is low\" is meaningless; compare implied vol with your forecast of realised vol.
  • Selling options earns the volatility risk premium most of the time and loses big in crashes (Part 14.2's doubling-strategy lesson again).
  • Sticky strike vs sticky delta: how the smile moves with spot changes the true delta.
  • Local and stochastic volatility models (Dupire, Heston) fit the smile; know they exist and why.
