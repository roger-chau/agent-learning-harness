Capstone · Fused attention
Everything in one kernel: tiling (Part 4), reductions and shuffles (Part 3), online softmax (Part 7), load balance (Part 5), and the compiler's view of the result (Part 6).

Build

Write runAttention(Q, K, V, O, N, d, causal): the attention forward pass, O = softmax(QKᵀ / √d) V, for N × d row-major FP32 matrices with d = 32, returning the array of stats for every launch it makes. With causal true, query i attends only to keys j ≤ i.

The starter contains the naive version: three kernels (scores to memory, row softmax, then P·V). Yours must never write the N × N matrix to DRAM.

How it's checked

  • Exact: within 10⁻⁵ of a reference, at N = 128 and 256, causal and not.
  • IO-aware: DRAM traffic at N = 256 at most 1.5 × the bytes of Q, K, V and O.
  • Fast: at N = 256, at least 3× faster than the naive three-kernel version (76,752 cycles).
  • Causal pays off: causal at most 0.8× your own non-causal time. Skipping masked blocks isn't enough on its own: think about which SM gets which work (Chapter 5.4). Stretch: 0.65×.

The report (the write-up)

This is the part an interviewer or a team lead would read. Cover:

  1. Design: tile sizes, thread mapping, what lives in registers and shared memory, and why.
  2. Profiling: the speed of light per unit, the limiter, bank conflicts and balance, before and after your main optimisations (Part 5's procedure).
  3. Compiler view: the instruction mix of your inner loop (FMAs vs everything else), what a compiler would do with it, and what you'd hand-schedule if you could (Part 6).
  4. Against the state of the art: what FlashAttention-2 and -3 do that you didn't, and which of those the simulator could or couldn't show.
