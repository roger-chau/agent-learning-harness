Why this lab

Part 10's patterns, on your card. The simulator showed that tiling a convolution cuts L2 traffic about 12× but barely changes the time, because the cache absorbs the reuse. Real GPUs have bigger L1 caches and a read-only path, so does that still hold? And sparse matrix–vector products are where "coalescing" stops being a textbook word: here you measure what a warp per row buys on a million-row matrix.

What to do

Open labs/L10-patterns/l10.cu.

  • A. Convolution (4096 × 4096, 5 × 5 filter in __constant__ memory). Write conv_tiled: 16 × 16 threads per block, a 20 × 20 input tile with halo in shared memory (zeros outside the image), a barrier, then the 25 FMAs from shared memory. Pad the tile's rows by one float, and work out why that matters here.
  • B. SpMV (2²⁰ rows of 32–95 non-zeros, CSR). Write spmv_vector: a warp per row, lanes striding through the row, a shuffle reduction. The given spmv_scalar uses a thread per row.

Both checks are exact: the data are small integers, so every sum is exact in FP32 whatever the order.

Predict, then measure

  • A: count global loads per output for each version (25 versus 400/256). Is either version memory-bound on your card, or compute-bound at 50 FLOPs per output? Measure L1 and L2 hit rates of conv_naive with make ncu: how much of its reuse do the caches already capture?
  • B: for the scalar kernel, how many sectors does a warp touch per step? For the vector kernel? Predict the speedup from sectors alone, then measure sectors per request with Nsight Compute.

Going further

  • A: try a 7 × 7 and a 9 × 9 filter. Tiling should gain more as R grows: does it? Then try a separable 9 × 9 (two 1-D passes).
  • B: build a matrix with skewed rows (most with 4 non-zeros, a few with 100,000). Both kernels degrade; implement merge-based SpMV (split non-zeros evenly, binary-search rowPtr) and compare.
  • Compare with cuSPARSE's cusparseSpMV (link -lcusparse) on both matrices.

Passing

All four checks pass; the tiled convolution is at least 1.1× faster than the naive one; the vector SpMV reaches at least 40% of peak bandwidth and is at least 1.5× faster than the scalar one. The report compares your predictions with the measurements and the profiler's cache hit rates.
