Scenario · Divergent physics
Your team's particle simulation step is too slow, and the profiler says warps are mostly idle.
Context
A game-physics prototype updates 20,000 particles per frame. Each particle is in one of three states: idle (about 60%), drifting (about 30%) or colliding (about 10%). The states are scattered randomly through the array.
The cost
Updating a particle runs physics(v, trips), a loop of 0, 8 or 64 iterations depending on its state.
Goal
Produce exactly the same results in at most half the simulated cycles of the baseline kernel, measured as the total over all the launches you make.
Constraints
Don't change physics or TRIPS. You may add launches, buffers and JavaScript code on the "host" (outside kernels); if you do host-side work, discuss its cost in your write-up.

Starting point

Run the starter's main() and read the baseline's stats. Before changing anything, write down what you think the bottleneck is and why. Chapter 1.4's loop-divergence animation is the picture to have in mind: with 10% of particles colliding, almost every warp contains at least one colliding lane ($1 - 0.9^{32} \approx 97\%$).

There's more than one way

Approaches people actually use for this kind of problem include:

  • reordering work so particles with the same state share warps (by index list, or by physically reordering the data);
  • one launch per state over a compacted list of indices;
  • spreading expensive particles' work over several threads.

Each has costs the others don't: extra memory traffic, extra launches, gathers that hurt coalescing, host-side work. Pick based on measurement, and say why in the write-up. Part 3 gives you GPU primitives (compaction, sorting) that would replace any host-side preprocessing. For now, assume it's acceptable but not free.

Your runParticles(state, x, out, n) must return an array of the stats of every launch you make, so the check can total them honestly.
