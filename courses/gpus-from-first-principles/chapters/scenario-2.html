Scenario · Edge camera ISP
A camera image-processing stage on an embedded board misses its frame budget.
Context
Your product runs a small image-signal-processing (ISP) stage on an SoC's integrated GPU before frames go to a vision model. The GPU is the GW-E: 4 SMs at 1.0 GHz, 51.2 GB/s of shared LPDDR bandwidth, 300-cycle memory latency and a 128 KB L2. It's much smaller than the GW-1.
The stage
For each 640×360 frame of 16-bit raw pixels: (1) subtract a black level of 64 (clamping at 0) and multiply by a gain; (2) a 3×3 box denoise with clamp-to-edge borders; (3) tone-map through a 1,024-entry 8-bit lookup table, after rounding and clamping to [0, 1023]. The output is an 8-bit image.
Today
Three kernels, with 32-bit float intermediates written to and read back from DRAM. That takes about 170 µs of GPU time per frame.
Goal
≤ 90 µs of total GPU time per frame (all launches), with output identical to the reference. Stretch: ≤ 65 µs.
Constraints
Your runISP(dev, raw, lut, w, h, gain) receives the GW-E device as dev; use dev.alloc and dev.launch, not gpu. Return { out, stats: [ … every launch … ] }.

Think before you code

Count the bytes. Per pixel, the baseline reads 2 bytes of raw data, writes and reads back two 4-byte float images, and writes 1 byte of output. That's at least 19 bytes of DRAM traffic per pixel, even with perfect caching. What does the minimum look like? Where does the roofline put this stage on the GW-E? Is it memory-bound at all once you've fixed the obvious problem?

Things to weigh

  • Fusion removes intermediate traffic, but a fused 3×3 stencil re-reads neighbours. Is that DRAM traffic or L2 traffic here?
  • Tiling in shared memory removes the re-reads, but adds a barrier and a halo.
  • Small data types (16-bit inputs, 8-bit outputs) mean each warp instruction moves only 64 or 32 bytes. That's many instructions per byte, and what does that do to which resource you're limited by?
  • The lookup table is 1 KB of random-access reads per pixel. Where should it live?

This is the kind of trade-off you'll make on Jetson-class hardware in the Embedded GPUs course. The numbers differ there, but the reasoning is the same.
