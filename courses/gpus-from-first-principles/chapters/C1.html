Project C · GEMM, naive to tensor cores · Milestone 1 of 5
Project C takes one kernel, $C = A \times B$ for square FP32 matrices, from naive to tensor cores, then (in Part 6) down to hand-scheduled assembly. Keep every version: the optimisation log in C3 compares them.

Build: a shared-memory tiled SGEMM

Write gemmTiled(A, B, C, n) using 16×16 shared-memory tiles as in Chapter 4.2, plus runTiled(A, B, C, n) with 16×16 blocks. Also keep a naive version, gemmNaive with runNaive(A, B, C, n), for comparison; your 4.1 kernel works. n is a multiple of 16.

How it's checked

  • Correct on 32×32 and 128×128.
  • Uses shared memory, all FMAs via fma().
  • Global traffic per FMA (L2 bytes moved ÷ FMAs) at least 4× lower than your naive kernel's on 128×128.
  • FMA share of issued instructions at least 27%. The naive kernel manages about 24%; a tiled kernel whose inner loop the compiler can't unroll drops to about 22%.
