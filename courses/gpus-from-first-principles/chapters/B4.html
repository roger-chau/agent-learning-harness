Project B · Parallel primitives · Milestone 4 of 5
A GPU radix sort for 32-bit unsigned keys, built from your own primitives: histogram, scan and a stable scatter. Correctness first; B5 (after Part 5's profiling chapters) makes it fast.

Build: radixSort

Write radixSort(keys, n), where keys is a u32 device buffer. Return { sorted, stats }: sorted is a u32 buffer holding the keys in ascending order, and stats lists every launch. A standard design per digit pass:

  1. Histogram each tile's digits into a table counts[digit × tiles + tile], digit-major, so one scan of the whole table gives every (digit, tile) its global offset.
  2. Exclusive scan the table. Your B2 scan, or anything correct: the table is small.
  3. Stable scatter: each key goes to its (digit, tile) offset plus its rank among same-digit keys earlier in its tile. Warp ballots (Chapter 3.5) give the rank within a warp; per-warp counts in shared memory give the rest.

Choose your digit width. Four bits means 16 bins and 8 passes; eight bits means 256 bins and 4 passes. Ping-pong between two buffers.

How it's checked

  • Sorted output on random keys, all-equal keys, already sorted, reverse sorted, a few distinct values, and keys near 0xFFFFFFFF (watch your shifts: use >>>, not >>).
  • Sizes 16,384, 1,000 and 1.
  • Array.prototype.sort and typed-array sort are disabled during the checks. The GPU does the sorting.
