Project B builds a small library of parallel primitives: reduce, scan, histogram, then radix sort built only from them. B5 profiles the whole library. Keep each milestone's code; later ones reuse it.

Build: reduceSum

Write reduceSum(x, n), which returns { sum, stats: [ … every launch … ] }, where sum is the total of the n floats in x. Use as many launches as you like, but no host-side summing of the input: the GPU must do the reduction. Reading a handful of per-block partials back on the host is fine.

What "fast" means here

A reduction is memory-bound with intensity ~1/4 FLOP per byte, so the target is bandwidth. The check computes your effective bandwidth as $4n$ bytes divided by the total simulated time of all your launches, and needs it to be ≥ 75% of the GW-1's 512 GB/s on $n = 2^{20}$.

The chapter has all the ingredients: a machine-sized launch with a grid-stride loop, warp shuffles, one shared-memory round per block, and a cheap way to combine block results. If you're stuck below the target, look at limitedBy and at occupancy. How many warps did you actually launch?

How it's checked

  • Correct sum (relative error ≤ 10⁻⁴) for several sizes, including $n = 1$ and $n$ not a power of two.
  • Effective bandwidth ≥ 75% of peak for $n = 2^{20}$.
