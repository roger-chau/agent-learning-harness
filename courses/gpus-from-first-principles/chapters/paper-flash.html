The problem

Attention's cost grows with the square of the sequence length, and at the time, most work on long sequences tried to cut its FLOPs with approximations (sparse or low-rank attention). They often didn't run faster in practice. The paper's diagnosis is the one from Chapter 7.2: standard attention is bound by memory traffic, writing and reading the N × N matrices, not by FLOPs. Counting FLOPs was the wrong model of the machine.

The key idea

Make attention IO-aware: tile it so the N × N matrices never leave on-chip memory, and pay for that with a little recomputation.

  • Tiling with online softmax (Chapter 7.2): load blocks of K and V into SRAM, compute a block of scores, update a running max, sum and output per query row. Exact, not approximate.
  • IO complexity: with on-chip memory of size M, standard attention moves Θ(Nd + N²) elements to and from HBM; FlashAttention moves Θ(N²d²/M). For typical d and M, that's many times fewer.
  • Recomputation in the backward pass: instead of storing the N × N probabilities for the gradient, store only the per-row statistics (max and sum) and recompute the scores block by block. More FLOPs, far less memory traffic, and a net speedup.

Block sizes follow from the on-chip memory: in the paper's Algorithm 1, Bc = ⌈M / 4d⌉ and Br = min(⌈M / 4d⌉, d).

Reading guide

  • FlashAttention-1, Section 3 and Algorithm 1: the forward pass; check each step against your Chapter 7.2 code. Then the IO-complexity theorem and its lower bound.
  • FlashAttention-2: three changes, all things you've met in this course. Fewer non-matmul FLOPs (rescale the output once at the end, not every block); parallelism over the sequence length as well as batch and heads (Part 5's tails: one head of a long sequence must still fill the GPU); and work partitioning across warps that splits Q rather than K, so warps don't have to exchange partial results through shared memory. The result was roughly twice FA-1's speed.
  • FlashAttention-3 (Hopper): asynchrony. A producer warp feeds tiles with TMA (Chapter 4.4) while consumer warpgroups run wgmma; "ping-pong" scheduling overlaps one warpgroup's softmax with another's GEMMs, since exp throughput is far below tensor-core throughput; and FP8 support with techniques to keep the error down.

Reproduce it

Do the IO arithmetic: block sizes from on-chip memory, and the HBM traffic of standard and Flash attention for real sizes. The capstone then builds the forward pass on the simulator.

What happened next

FlashAttention became the default attention implementation in the major training and inference stacks, and its IO-aware approach became a template: count bytes, not FLOPs; tile so the big intermediate stays on-chip; recompute when that's cheaper than storing. Variants cover decoding (split-KV, "flash-decoding"), paged KV caches (Part 7), and other GPUs and accelerators. For a GPU performance or compiler engineer, it's the canonical case study of everything this course covers in one kernel.
