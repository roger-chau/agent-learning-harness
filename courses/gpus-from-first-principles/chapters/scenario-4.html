Scenario · LLM decode
A small language model is too slow at generating text, and the team wants to know why, and what batching would buy.
Context
While generating, a transformer produces one token at a time, and each token runs every layer's projections as a matrix-vector product: $y = W x$. You're given one such projection: $W$ is 512 × 512 FP32, row-major (row o holds the weights of output o). Serving many users at once means batch independent vectors share the same $W$.
Inputs
W (512·512 floats), X (batch × 512, row-major: one vector per row), Y (batch × 512, output). batch is between 1 and 8.
Goal
batch 1 in ≤ 4,000 cycles and batch 8 in ≤ 11,000 cycles in total, with $Y$ within 1e-4 relative error. Stretch: batch 8 in ≤ 7,000 cycles.
Interface
decode(W, X, Y, batch) returns an array of the stats of every launch it made.

Before you write code

Estimate the floors. How long does it take just to read $W$ once from DRAM at 512 GB/s? How many FMAs does batch 8 need, and how long do they take at 1,536 GFLOP/s? Compute the arithmetic intensity (FLOPs per byte of weights) for batch 1 and batch 8, and place both on the GW-1 roofline. One of them is on the other side of the ridge, and that changes what you should optimise.

Things that will bite

  • Splitting a row's dot product across a warp means a reduction at the end. Count what it costs when a warp holds many sums.
  • $X$ is small, but if every warp reads all of it, that's a lot of L2 traffic. Who should read which part of $X$, and how often?
  • The L2 is 256 KB and $W$ is 1 MB. Reading $W$ once per vector costs $8\times$ as much as reading it once.

There's no single right design. Measure, read limitedBy, and explain your choices in the write-up.
