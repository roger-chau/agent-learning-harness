Where you are

You can explain why GPUs exist and how a warp executes; predict a kernel's limit from its bytes, FLOPs and parallelism; write reductions, scans, sorts, GEMMs and attention that approach the hardware's limits; measure honestly and diagnose from evidence; read and reason about generated code like a compiler; and build systems around kernels: streams, serving, paged memory, numerics, portability and power. That's the working core of both jobs you're aiming at.

Real hardware

Everything here ran on a simulator built to be honest about first-order effects. The next step is real GPUs: rewrite the capstone in CUDA or Triton, profile it with Nsight Compute, and find what the simulator didn't model (instruction-level stalls, the real cache hierarchy, clocks). A cloud instance or a Jetson-class board is enough. The gaps you find are worth writing up: that's how engineers show they understand a machine.

Keeping up

  • Conferences: ISCA, MICRO, HPCA (architecture); PPoPP, SC (parallel computing); CGO, PLDI, ASPLOS (compilers and systems); MLSys and OSDI/SOSP (ML systems). Hot Chips for what vendors are building.
  • Vendor documentation: each new architecture's tuning guide and ISA notes, and microbenchmark studies when they appear (Part 5).
  • Code: release notes and pull requests of the projects above. That's where new techniques land first.
  • H&P: you've now covered the GPU-relevant chapters. Chapters 2 (memory hierarchy), 5 (multiprocessors) and 7 (domain-specific architectures) reward a second read now.
