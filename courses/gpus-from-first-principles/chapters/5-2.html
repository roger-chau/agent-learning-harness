The real problem

A profiler like Nsight Compute reports hundreds of metrics per kernel. Reading them all is hopeless; reading the wrong one is worse. A performance engineer starts from a handful that answer one question, which part of the machine is this kernel using up?, and only then drills into the details.

The smallest idea: speed of light

For each unit, compare what the kernel asked of it with what the unit could have done in the same time. That ratio is the unit's "speed of light" (SOL) percentage, and Nsight Compute's first section shows it for compute and memory:

$$\text{SOL}_{\text{DRAM}} = \frac{\text{DRAM bytes} / \text{peak bytes per cycle}}{\text{kernel cycles}}$$

A unit near 100% is the bottleneck; making anything else faster won't help. On the GW-1:

The simulator reports these as stats.sol. The warp instructions it counts are memInstructions + branches + sharedWavefronts + barriers + warpOps + (atomicSerialization − atomics) + fmaInstructions.

Add one block: the metrics behind the SOL

When a unit is saturated, the next question is whether it's doing useful work. A few ratios answer that:

  • Sectors per request (transactions / memInstructions): 4 for a coalesced warp of 4-byte loads, up to 32 for scattered ones. High DRAM SOL with high sectors per request means bandwidth spent on bytes nobody wanted (Part 2).
  • Arithmetic intensity (flops / dramBytes): where the kernel sits on the roofline.
  • Achieved bandwidth (dramBytes / time), and the L2 hit rate.
  • FMA share of issued instructions: is the issue-bound kernel issuing math, or overhead?
  • Achieved occupancy and bank conflicts: the usual suspects when nothing is saturated (next chapter).

The whole machine: stall reasons

Real profilers also sample why warps aren't issuing, each cycle. The common names, and what they point at:

Stall reason (Nsight Compute)Usually means
Long scoreboardWaiting on global or local memory: latency or bandwidth
Short scoreboard / MIO throttleWaiting on shared memory: often bank conflicts
Math pipe throttleThe FP32 or tensor units are busy: compute-bound (good)
BarrierWaiting at __syncthreads: imbalance inside the block
Not selectedEnough eligible warps; the scheduler picked another. Healthy

The simulator doesn't sample stalls, but its time model has the same structure: the largest SOL decides limitedBy, and when every SOL is low, the kernel is waiting on latency.

Reality

  • Profilers replay the kernel several times to collect all counters, so profiled runs are slower and must not be used for timing.
  • Percentages are relative to peaks the hardware rarely reaches: 80–90% of DRAM peak is excellent in practice.
  • Learn your profiler's speed-of-light, memory-workload and warp-state sections first. They cover most real diagnoses.
