Paper project · Dissecting a GPU
You're handed a GPU with no spec sheet. Measure it.

The setup

Write dissect(dev). The tests call it on GW-1 variants whose spec is hidden and different from the documented GW-1. dev has alloc, from and launch, but launch returns only { cycles }: a stopwatch, nothing else. Compile your kernels with the global gpu.compile; they run on any device.

Things you know (from the earlier Parts)

  • Time = the largest of the DRAM, L2, issue and tensor times, plus one DRAM latency. DRAM time is bandwidth-limited, or latency-limited when too few bytes are in flight (Little's law). Use that model to design each experiment so that the quantity you want is the only thing that changes.
  • Each launch starts with an empty L2. Blocks run in index order.
  • Difference measurements cancel fixed costs: time with 33 loads minus time with 1 load, rather than time with 33 loads.
  • Make sure the regime you think you're in is the one you're in. A sweep that's latency-limited at small sizes and bandwidth-limited at large ones has a "knee" that has nothing to do with caches.

Then explain

The write-up asks for each microbenchmark: what it varies, why only the target quantity affects it, and what could have fooled it. That last part is the real skill: the authors of the Volta paper spent most of their effort ruling out wrong explanations.
